Topic Content — Common Across All Titles

Context-dependent design: environment/surroundings, user requirements, economic/market considerations, product opportunities
Stakeholder analysis methods: user-centred design, SWOT analysis, focus groups, qualitative observations, market research
Enterprise/innovation routes: entrepreneurship, commercial partnerships, venture capitalists, crowd funding
Usability: impact on lifestyle, ease of use/inclusivity, ergonomics and anthropometric data, aesthetics
Anthropometric data for design parameters; user comfort, layout of controls, software UI
2. Learning from Existing Products and Practice
Analyse existing products: context, materials/components, construction/manufacture, functionality, ergonomics, inclusivity, fitness for purpose, lifestyle impact, trends/taste/style, marketing/branding, route to market
New and emerging technologies — influence on product evolution and innovation
Past and present designers/technologies: impact on industry/enterprise, lifestyle/culture/society, environment, sustainability
Key historical movements and figures
Product marketing lifecycle: initial demand, growth, decline, methods to extend popularity, social media influence
3. Implications of Wider Issues

Environmental impact: sourcing/processing raw materials, waste disposal, pollution, cost implications
Sustainability: fair trade, ETI, globalisation, material optimisation, recycling, eco-materials, up-cycling
Optimum material use: cost, stock sizes/forms, sustainable production
Distribution: cost-effective distribution, environmental issues, social media/mobile tech, global production
IP: registered designs, trademarks, copyright, design rights, patents
Cross-disciplinary knowledge: maths, science, primary/secondary research, specialist publications
4. Design Thinking and Communication
Graphical communication: annotated 2D/3D sketching, digital tools, sketch modelling
Digital tools: online collaboration, CAD, rendering, simulation, photo-quality imaging
Design approaches: iterative designing, user-centred design, circular economy, systems thinking
Collaboration across subject areas
Project management: critical path analysis, scrum, six sigma
7. Manufacturing Processes and Techniques
Iterative models:

Block models, working prototypes, hand tools, digital tools, rapid prototyping, digital simulation
Scales of production:

NEA — Iterative Design Project
Core Process: Explore → Create → Evaluate (iterative)
Explore (AO1): Systematically understand stakeholder requirements — who, what, when, where, why, impact on society/environment/economics. Direct personal interaction with stakeholders.

Create (AO2): Generate novel and appropriate ideas. Avoid design fixation. Use conceptual combination. Progress from quick freehand sketches → detailed iterations → technical specifications → final prototype. Digital design/manufacture must be used.

Evaluate (AO3): Test solutions against stakeholder requirements. Where needs unmet, further explore/create. Repeat until requirements satisfied. Select appropriate systematic testing techniques.

NEA Content Requirements
Identifying requirements — investigate contexts, develop brief, determine stakeholder requirements
Learning from existing products — analyse existing products (destructive/non-destructive), aesthetics/ergonomics/anthropometrics, design theory/history
Implications of wider issues — social/moral/ethical factors, cross-disciplinary knowledge, environmental/lifecycle analysis
Design thinking and communication — technical/non-technical specifications, user-centred design, range of media/presentation techniques, 2D/3D sketching/modelling/CAD, reports, presentations
Material considerations — select appropriate materials/components, material combinations
Technical understanding — functionality/usability requirements, appropriate materials/components/finishes/technology, aesthetics/ergonomics/anthropometrics
Manufacturing — marking out, templates/jigs/patterns, tolerances, specialist tools/equipment/machinery, experimentation/testing/modelling, hand/machine/digital technologies, manufacturing methods/scales/quality, DFMA
Viability — final prototype(s) meeting requirements, test/evaluate against requirements with stakeholders, commercial viability/cost/marketability, physical testing, IT-based testing, stakeholder feedback, BSI/ISO standards
Health and safety — hazard identification, risk assessments, safe working

Endorsement-Specific NEA Interpretation
Design Engineering: Focus on functional requirements and systems. Systems approach. Full-scale modelling for ergonomic/dimensional/functional suitability. Video evidence of functionality. CAD/CAM/CAE expected. Technical/working drawings for commercial viability.

Fashion and Textiles: Wide-ranging textile products. Fashions/trends, industrial/commercial practices. Reverse engineering. Toiles/samples/pattern drafting. Laser cutter, e-textiles, sublimation printer, 3D printer expected. Pattern drafting, lay plans, working drawings required. Marketing aspects throughout.

Product Design: Broad domestic/commercial/industrial contexts. Disassembly and comparison of products. Storage, transport, maintenance considerations. CAD for final solutions (orthographic, rendered). Marketing aspects throughout. Animation for articulation/structural analysis.
